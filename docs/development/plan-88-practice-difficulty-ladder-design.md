## Requirement 0 — Propose and stop

Run preflight and pin the baseline. Propose two or three existing families for detailed worked comparisons, then outline the coverage review across the current managed-practice families. Identify the task features that may change their demands, the evidence to inspect, and what would falsify a plausible ordering. Propose disjoint investigation assignments and where the lead will reconcile their findings. Save a short scope proposal under `reports/development/plan-88-practice-difficulty-ladder-design/`. Stop for orchestration approval before the investigation.

## Design and deliver

The owner requested a broader managed mix after reviewing the initial two-family
proposal. Treat that comparison-to-distance ladder as one starting pattern.
Design several complete visits across public families, including magnitude and
common-unit or equivalence patterns. Show why the tasks belong together, how
demand changes within each goal, and how direct Start varies visits without a
required menu or learner profile. Map both current and proposed managed families
to exact public records, fit evidence or gaps, and explicit exclusions. The
successor delivers every adopted pattern through its internal gates.

1. Compare the same bank under a short seeded draw and a modest authored progression. Consider features such as exchanging a whole, the relation to a familiar benchmark, and whether the learner constructs or evaluates an equivalent form. Explain counterexamples to a universal denominator-based ranking. Keep task demands, available help and viewport eligibility separate.
2. Show concrete short journeys with variation within a comparable level and a reason for each change in demand. Keep Start a practice session a direct launch. Describe how the progression remains coherent if the wider pool is unavailable, and how a learner can move at their own pace using existing controls or one separately proposed optional action. Make assumptions and decisions inspectable. Use no learner profile or inference of mastery.
3. Specify the complete ladder recommended for managed practice. Define the levels or ordering rules, entry and end, variation within levels, and the conditions for holding, raising or lowering demand within a planned sequence. Explain the role of any learner pacing control. Map each current managed-practice family to supported coverage, a conservative placement, or a reason for exclusion. Distinguish evidence-backed judgments from hypotheses. Show how full sessions play out with a normal pool, a narrow or enlarged-text pool, and too few eligible records. No persistent profile or automatic inference of mastery.
4. Deliver a design and owner decision brief plus an implementation brief for **one successor packet that can deliver the whole adopted ladder**. Specify task-feature ownership and data contracts, deterministic ordering and reproduction, selection and fit fallback, direct launch through completion, legacy route and episode replay compatibility, and mounted acceptance journeys. Use gates inside that packet to review the mechanism, validate the ordering and eligibility across batches, then review integration. A first batch can test the mechanism. The intended delivery includes all adopted levels and families, integrated end to end, with exclusions and reasons documented. Identify which gates orchestration can clear within the adopted scope and which changes need an owner decision. Preserve owner rendered acceptance and separate approval for default exposure of held content or deployment.
5. The Terra-high lead may dispatch Luna-medium workers for disjoint, report-only investigations and checks. The lead reconciles findings, owns the design and implementation brief, and reports at gates. Children do not delegate. Propose the same lead-and-worker model for the successor, with single ownership of shared scheduler, registration and replay edits and serialized integration. Validate references and packet lint, and commit `progress.md` last. Keep runtime changes, new data collection, classroom testing and deployment outside Plan 88.

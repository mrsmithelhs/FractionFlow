## What needs a decision

For `1/2 + 3/8`, the canonical unit 8 requires only the left operand renamed.
The authored alternative 16 requires both; 24 is also mathematically valid and
representation-eligible but lacks authored path coverage. The mirrored case swaps
the required side. Plan 16 freezes the schedule at construction from canonical
renaming flags (DECISION-034). Existing correct-common-unit treatment does not
make a non-least valid denominator a mathematical error (DECISION-028).

The engine does not currently admit this family, so this is a prospective nested
task conflict, distinct from the live denominator-36 defect assigned to draft Plan 22.
One-sided and zero-sided schedule construction tests do not prove their reducers
or reflection subjects can execute.

A pre-scheduled two-conversion arc with a dynamic no-op bypass is another proposed
structural approach, not a loophole already permitted by DECISION-034. It needs an
explicit account of skipped schedule entries, visible work and replay; do not auto-
submit a fictitious conversion. A learner-selected alternate path is not automatically
performance adaptation, but response-derived traversal still conflicts with the
current construction contract. Reconsider the contract on its merits rather than
use the word "adaptive" as the sole argument.

## Recommendation and evidence required

Do not select an option through implementation convenience. First decide whether
general common-unit choice is part of the intended nested practice or whether a
focused side/unit task is the better increment. Then approve the smallest mechanism
that realizes that task. No winner is declared here.

Any mechanism brief must trace 8, 16 and 24, plus both operand orders; show effective
operand forms at operate; identify the reflection subject by the actual task rather
than always "left"; preserve exact mathematics and distinguish missing authored
coverage from ineligible drawing. Compare the retained and lost learner decisions,
prompt density, support parity and replay identity. Name an observation that would
show the narrowed task fails the desired instructional goal.

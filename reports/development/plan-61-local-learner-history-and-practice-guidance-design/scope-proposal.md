The main dossier would compare optional remembering modes, shared-profile contamination/reset, data minimization, compatibility and failure cases, and at most two explainable future selection approaches. It would separate normalized observations, tentative summaries and policy; recommend one modest descriptive slice and explicitly gate adaptive selection. Synthetic cases test no data, assistance, repetition versus variation, contradiction, mixed users, changed definitions and erasure/corruption. A design fails if it promotes sparse or mixed evidence to a confident skill claim, silently strengthens old evidence after a cue change, or makes practice unavailable when history is absent.

## Targeted research proposal

First inspect the existing Plan 29 source/research table and the project evidence posture; reuse relevant sources and their limits. Then seek **three to six total** primary studies or authoritative syntheses, including existing sources where suitable, to answer only these decision-changing questions:

1. **Formative feedback:** What descriptive, actionable feedback can be supported without turning a short history into an ability label? Candidate: Hattie and Timperley, *The Power of Feedback* (2007); check original scope and limitations.
2. **Inference and assistance/transfer:** How do task cues, assistance and varied opportunities constrain a claim of independent understanding? Candidate: National Research Council, *Knowing What Students Know* (2001), assessment/inference framework; identify a relevant primary assistance or transfer study if the existing Plan 29 evidence does not answer this.
3. **Retrieval, spacing and interleaving:** What cautious design hypotheses for later review and variation are warranted, and when might practice-stage or task differences matter? Candidates: Roediger and Karpicke (2006) on retrieval; Rohrer, Dedrick and Stershic (2015) on interleaved mathematics practice; verify original methods and populations before inclusion.

These are candidates, not claims of applicability or a preselected bibliography. Prefer school-age mathematics or fraction evidence where directly relevant; identify age, task, setting, comparison, outcome, duration and limits for every load-bearing source. Adult verbal-memory experiments, classroom scheduling studies and synthesis findings do not validate FractionFlow's interface, an individual learner's mastery, a numeric threshold, an optimal interval or fixed session mixture. No new learner data or efficacy study is authorized here.

**Stopping rule:** stop once existing evidence plus a small targeted set can distinguish defensible descriptive wording, assistance/uncertainty limits and a cautious future selection direction. If a material question remains unresolved, report the gap and recommend waiting or a separately scoped investigation; do not expand into a general literature survey. Request orchestration approval before exceeding six sources or materially changing the inventory.

## Compare and recommend

**A — independent [0,2] locate, then bar verification (recommended).** The learner must locate an improper fraction across the one-whole boundary, recognizing the second interval as another same-sized whole. It extends existing [0,1] placement by making 1 an interior boundary and requiring continuity into a second unit; it is not just a longer line. A second representation verifies the established quantity. It does not require formal mixed-number conversion.

**B — construct whole plus fractional part, then connect to improper form.** This directly foregrounds grouping and could expose how a whole is built from equal fractional units. It asks a different and potentially valuable learner question, but needs a decision about which notation is supplied, what is constructed, and whether the connection itself is established; it introduces more new notation/interaction at once. Neither prior task acceptance nor exact math determines which approach teaches better. A is the smallest testable bridge to the specific stable-boundary gap; this is a design judgment, not a research finding. If the owner needs a comparative teaching claim before choosing, first frame the question as whether line-first or unit-construction-first improves recognition of equivalent quantities above one, then seek at most three directly relevant primary empirical studies and report population, task and transfer limits. No research was needed to make this bounded engineering recommendation.

## Proposed one-item journey and rules

Use one supplied original improper fraction at a time, including proper/whole boundary contrasts in a short curated sequence. Proposed initial authored denominator set 2, 4, 8; numerator 0 through 2d, with a small explicitly vetted set rather than automatic admission of all fractions. This follows existing ≤8 source capability but is **not** proof that a two-whole line with 17 eighth positions is usable; shrink the set if rendered/access checks fail. Preserve original numerator and denominator. Benchmarks 0, 1, 2 remain labeled. For 5/4, learner selects an initially unplaced position, checks, and can revise. Only acceptance establishes the location; the source form was supplied, not derived. After acceptance, a two-row bar shows one full whole and one quarter, and the accepted point. A plain informational “1 whole and 1/4” may accompany the resolved picture; it is not a learner-established mixed form. Omit a zero remainder for whole-only display. Never auto-label an unestablished mixed numeral as an answer. No formal mixed input is required.

**Proposed geometry:** if line endpoints are x0 and x2, x1=(x0+x2)/2; one unit is x1−x0, not the full line width. Each whole bar has that same unit length; fractional segments have consistent whole/denominator scale. Exact candidate i/d is placed at x0+(i/(2d))·(x2−x0); i=d is exactly 1 and i=2d exactly 2. Use the DECISION-032 vertical stack: both bar rows begin at x0 and end at x1, each one whole wide. For a value 1 + r/d, the second row shades from x0 to x0+(r/d)(x1−x0), while the cumulative line point is x1+(r/d)(x1−x0). These endpoints are one whole apart, so do not draw an endpoint-alignment connector. The bar rows are a countable verification, not a spatially continuous extension of the line. At 1 the first row is full and there is no shaded second row; at 2 both rows are full. If the layout cannot maintain this scale and readable controls, use sequential views with an explicit unit reference, never a stretched or misleading connector. Exact values govern correctness upstream, never pixels. These are acceptance criteria, not measured current behavior.

**Journey 1:** `5/4` supplied. Initially no marker position. Learner chooses `4/4` at 1 and checks: “Check the placement.” The marker stays editable and feedback is associated with its control. They choose the next fourth and check: “That's right.” The bar appears with one full row and one quarter; Next is immediately available. Requested help before acceptance may highlight the 0–1 and 1–2 intervals and state that they are equal wholes; it must not mark the target. Record help as assistance. Recommend identical default support for More and Less in this small pilot: the same 0/1/2 labels, equal partition ticks and exact candidate choices. This avoids an untested support difference while retaining requested help and the same accessible candidate meanings in both settings. Help never places the answer.

**Journey 2:** `4/4` supplied. Learner initially selects the endpoint at 2; feedback says “Check the placement.” They move to 1 and accept. Bar verification shows exactly one full unit; no phantom second shaded row, duplicate endpoint, or “1 whole and 0/4” is needed. Direct Next follows. Initial Check without a position gives neutral “Place the fraction first.” A tentative position is distinct from an incorrect checked position; revision clears stale error; accepted is distinct and locks response while preserving result.

Pointer/touch can choose a candidate via a sufficiently large non-drag target or select-and-place; keyboard and linear access select the same candidate positions with explicit spoken values, not coarse buckets, and Check remains separate.

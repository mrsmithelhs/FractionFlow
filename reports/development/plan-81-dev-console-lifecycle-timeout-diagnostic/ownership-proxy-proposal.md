## Actual ownership boundary

The targeted Vitest test executes `runConsole` in its test worker. The worker also owns an independent Vite server in-process. Its `OwnedServers` instance calls `spawnImpl` synchronously; the default `node:child_process.spawn` launches the Node helper directly with one IPC channel plus stdout/stderr pipes. The worker retains the `ChildProcess` handle and consumes `ready`, `start-error`, `stopped`, `stop-error`, `error`, and `exit`; it sends `{type:'stop'}` on that same channel. The helper loads Vite and hosts the listener in its own process. On IPC disconnect it attempts cooperative shutdown, but that code alone does not give an outer process proof of close or exit, particularly if startup or shutdown stalls. Other Vite/runtime descendants, if any, are not mapped or proved absent. The exact npm/Vitest launcher and worker topology and worker count were not established in the previous runs and must be observed in a future disposable setup, not assumed from this diagram.

```text
current, simplified: Vitest test worker ── owns IPC/handle ──> helper + Vite listener
                            └── independent Vite listener in worker process
```

The existing outer synthetic supervisor owns neither the actual worker's helper handle nor its IPC. A PID or port reported by the worker is identification evidence, **not authority to signal**, and PID lookup, process-name matching, port-owner termination, or a post-exit PID cannot safely repair that gap. The helper's disconnect handler is a useful cooperative fallback, not an externally verified cleanup result. A worker exit also destroys its in-process independent listener, so a separate control listener is needed to test noninterference; it does not replace the original assertion.

## Candidate boundary: supervisor-owned broker and worker-side proxy

In a verified disposable copy only, propose a run-scoped supervisor that directly spawns and retains live handles to the synthetic worker and each test helper. The instrumented worker's injected `spawnImpl` returns a synchronous ChildProcess-shaped proxy and requests the supervisor to launch the exact helper invocation. The supervisor owns the helper's real IPC, stdout/stderr streams, exit/error events and handle, and relays ordered events to the proxy; proxy `send` relays the exact message to that handle. A run ID, unguessable per-run channel capability, monotonically increasing request/sequence IDs, and an allowlist of the exact executable, helper path, arguments, cwd, environment subset and stdio shape prevent a request becoming a general process launcher. The worker cannot launch a helper independently through this instrumented seam. The supervisor registers the launch intent in a flushed ledger **before** spawning, retains the actual handle immediately, records spawn/error/exit before reporting launch success, and tracks the allocated port associated with that invocation. It must not promise that a pre-launch ledger alone eliminates the spawn/registration crash window: the live supervisor owns the handle; a supervisor crash is outside this proposal's safe recovery claim.

The proxy must preserve the specific synchronous interface that `OwnedServers` uses: `pid`/`connected`, `stdout`/`stderr` event streams, `send`, message/error/exit ordering, and launch-failure distinction. Because remote launch cannot supply a real PID synchronously, a placeholder or delayed update could change behavior. Do not claim equivalence by design: specify and test how the proxy presents pending launch state, buffering, send callbacks and disconnect events, then compare with direct launch. If faithful behavior cannot be demonstrated without changing assertions, timing, or lifecycle semantics, **stop at the existing evidence limit**, rather than use the proxy for the real test. A proxy observation is diagnostic evidence with measurable perturbation, not an unmodified baseline.

## Proposed controlled proxy proof — separate approval required

Use synthetic worker and helper only, a fresh run-specific disposable directory and fresh test-owned `127.0.0.1` listeners. Before any child or listener launch, create the run directory exclusively, validate resolved paths, reserve the run-specific raw trace, ledger and final output with exclusive creation, and verify they are writable. No fixed committed evidence path may be opened for the first time after launch. Retain run artifacts on failure and sanitize identities before any commit. Record actual handles and allocated ports in the private ledger; committed output uses labels. Establish the independent control listener with a separate owner/handle, keep it out of the helper stop list, and verify it remains reachable after cleanup before its owner closes it.

Propose at most **six sequential scenarios**, one attempt each: (1) normal completion; (2) worker remains alive but stops responding after requesting helper launch; (3) worker exits unexpectedly after helper readiness; (4) worker IPC disconnects while helper is live; (5) helper exits unexpectedly or reports startup failure; (6) a deliberate assertion failure after helper launch. Each has a 2-second synthetic worker deadline, a 3-second total cooperative cleanup budget, and bounded startup/handshake waits of at most 3 seconds per stage. Stop immediately after the first uncertain identity, unaccounted exit, failed cleanup, incomplete trace, unexpected collision, or scenario mismatch; do not run later scenarios. Record every started attempt, including censored or failed ones. Only after review of this proxy proof may a separate real-run plan be considered; no real test launches are part of this scope.

For scenarios 2–4, prove the supervisor can send stop via its own still-connected helper handle without the worker forwarding it, receive its acknowledgement when available, observe that exact helper's exit, and verify the specific test-owned listener is closed; independently observe the worker's exact exit or report it unresolved. A disconnected worker channel must block new launch requests; pending requests are resolved against the supervisor's authoritative ledger, never guessed from a PID. On startup failure or unexpected helper exit, record launch error versus exit, close any remaining owned handle cooperatively, and do not claim acknowledgement if none arrived. An IPC-disconnected helper, stop error, missing acknowledgement, unobserved exit, or a responding port after helper exit is **cleanup unverified**; stop and preserve evidence. A port check verifies listener state only and never authorizes signalling its occupant. Do not force-kill or probe unrelated listeners. A worker deliberately made unresponsive must have a separately designed cooperative way to exit (for example, a control channel independent of the simulated blocked work); otherwise report its exit as unverified and stop rather than assume the supervisor can stop it.

All exits from an attempt—including assertion failure, partial setup, logging failure, spawn failure and timeout—must enter an idempotent `finally` cleanup that attempts cooperative stop for **each already registered live owned handle independently**, even if another assertion or cleanup step fails. Bound the aggregate wait; record each request, acknowledgement, actual exit, listener check and error. A supervisor process must remain alive to perform that cleanup; an unresponsive owned process may prevent clean process termination, which must be surfaced as a stop for owner direction rather than concealed by a hard kill or a misleading success. Do not remove disposable evidence unless every owned resource is accounted for. In particular, the current `controlled-supervisor.cjs` is not a reusable safe runner: its fixed output is opened with `wx` only after child launches, and errors before the cleanup block can leave owned handles without a cooperative stop attempt. It must be replaced or repaired under the separately approved proof scope before reuse.

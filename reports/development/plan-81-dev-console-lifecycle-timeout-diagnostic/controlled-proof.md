# Controlled supervisor proof — review gate

2026-11-07. At baseline `2365fa299da075cb98f5edce9f5a31e18e243501`, preflight was RUNNABLE. This is a **synthetic proof**, not another attempt at the intermittent lifecycle test and not a proposed console fix. Stop for orchestration review before any real reproduction.

`node reports/development/plan-81-dev-console-lifecycle-timeout-diagnostic/controlled-supervisor.cjs` runs from the canonical root, creating a fresh report-local disposable directory that must not already exist. The supervisor uses only Node built-ins and runs its synthetic worker and helper as direct children with that directory as cwd. The helper binds a newly allocated `127.0.0.1` listener; a distinct independently owned control listener remains in the supervisor. A run-specific ledger with unlaunched slots is written and flushed before either child launch; the actual `ChildProcess` handles remain live in the supervisor, and actual PIDs and ports are recorded in the disposable ledger. Only those handles receive cooperative stop messages. No name-based or port-based process termination occurs. Raw ledger and trace are removed only after both runs finish successfully; committed evidence substitutes labels for PIDs and ports.

There were **four synthetic attempts**, in two sequential pairs. The [preliminary pair](controlled-proof-preliminary.json) passed both its normal and deliberately stalled scenarios, with clean exits and listener checks. Its timeout path waited for a deadline without racing an unexpected completion, so it is retained as preliminary evidence only. The final driver adds an actual completion-versus-deadline race; the [final pair](controlled-proof-evidence.json) is the proof result. No setup or cleanup uncertainty occurred in either pair. Each pair comprised one passing worker and one controlled timeout; no real test was launched.

The final passing worker signalled completion and both children acknowledged stop and exited with code 0. In the final controlled timeout, the ledger was established at 2.04 ms, helper and worker handles were recorded after their launches, the worker signalled `await-blocked` at 190.40 ms, and the supervisor recorded the 2,000 ms worker deadline at 2,109.57 ms relative to attempt start. The last completed phase was `await-blocked`; `work-complete` was the first missing event. After the deadline, both exact child handles were sent stop; both acknowledged and both emitted code-0 exit events. A bind to the helper's exact former port then succeeded and was closed, showing that listener was no longer bound at that point. The separate control listener was successfully connected **after** child cleanup and then closed by its owning supervisor; its port was subsequently bindable. The passing control completed the same cleanup and survival checks. The raw trace existed across the deadline and was converted to the sanitized event sequence only after cleanup.

The synthetic worker deadline is 2,000 ms from immediately before worker launch. Setup awaits are individually bounded at 3,000 ms; cleanup is bounded by one 3,000 ms budget from the stop phase. Each execution contains at most two attempts and stops at the first thrown error or uncertainty; it does not send an emergency kill. On failure the disposable ledger and trace are retained, and live handles are not replaced with guessed PIDs. A failed child or disconnected IPC is not treated as successful cleanup. File writes are synchronous and can perturb timing; the proof concerns event ordering and ownership, not performance. The real test's deadline remains 20 seconds and its assertions remain unchanged.

Limits: this demonstrates cooperative cleanup of **synthetic direct children** while the supervisor itself remains alive. It does not establish crash recovery, ownership of grandchildren, a safe response to an uncooperative child, or that a Vitest worker and its Vite descendants can be governed the same way. It does not account for, probe, or terminate any process or listener from the earlier uninstrumented timeout. The test-only control listener belongs to the supervisor rather than a separate process; its successful post-cleanup connection demonstrates it was left running through both stop requests. Further design and review are required before applying this mechanism to the real case.

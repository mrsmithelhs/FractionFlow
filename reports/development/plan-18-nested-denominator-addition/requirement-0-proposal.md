## Seven-step arc and the five-step alternative

The packet's actual seven-step arc is `encounter → notice → decide → transform-required-side → operate → resolve → reflect`. Its retained responsibilities are: acknowledge the expression; notice the denominator relationship; identify which fraction needs renaming; construct the equivalent fraction; combine like units; settle the result; and reflect on the completed mathematical relationship. The proposed five-step alternative is exactly `encounter → select-renaming-side → transform-required-side → operate → resolve`. It removes standalone Notice and Reflection while preserving the meaningful side decision and all mathematical work. The owner was asked whether to omit those two standalone steps; that choice is pending and neither omission is authorized yet.

If five steps are selected, the side-choice prompt is: **“Which fraction needs new parts to use eighths?”** Show the two original fractions in source order as stacked, selectable fraction choices, labeled in speech as “first fraction, one half” and “second fraction, three eighths” (with the fractions and ordinal labels swapped in the mirrored record). Do not color, emphasize, preselect, or otherwise distinguish the correct side before a response. The exact response wire shape is `{ "type": "select-renaming-side", "side": "left" }` or `{ "type": "select-renaming-side", "side": "right" }`; exact-key validation rejects any other key or side value. Add this exact intent to `INTENT_KEYS` with only `type` and `side`, map it through `INTENT_BEATS` to the new definition's `decide` position, and dispatch it only for the Plan18 definition. `propose-common-denominator` and `submit-notice` are rejected for the five-step definition; legacy definitions keep their current dispatch. Correctness is checked against the immutable authored `requiredSide`, but it does not change the already-frozen schedule. An invalid side value is rejected; a well-shaped wrong side gets local recovery and stays at the same side-selection position. Task-local help can say, “The unit is eighths. Compare each denominator with 8. Which fraction needs new parts?” It must not select a side or show a converted numerator.

The five-step arc is shorter and avoids a separate unit-relationship notice plus a later reflection step; its cost is losing an explicit opportunity to notice the denominator relationship and revisit the conversion/result after solving. The seven-step arc retains those pedagogical moments but risks making a compact, one-renaming task feel repetitive. This proposal records that tradeoff; the owner’s pending choice determines the final schedule and packet amendment. Do not infer that DECISION-042's compact Plan 34 patterns settle this question: Plan 34's construct/inspect grammar is specific to its new definitions and does not authorize shortening Plan 18.

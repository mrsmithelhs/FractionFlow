## Recommendation: place three numbers

Emphasize **equivalence as the same location**, with ordering an inherent consequence of placing all three, not a second lesson. Prompt: “Place each fraction on the number line. Some may belong at the same point.” Three supplied, stacked fraction labels begin in a separate tray. Learner produces three locations; the supplied line spans 0 to 1, with visible benchmarks and a common set of possible positions. Do not show an aligned answer bar or pre-place a token. One Check evaluates the complete collection. Use a mixture of equivalent pairs plus a contrast and all-distinct collections; defer an all-equivalent collection initially because the intended contrast is lost and this adds no new control capability. It is mathematically valid, not a duplicate-content error.

```text
Place each fraction on the number line.
Some may belong at the same point.
[ 3 ]     [ 1 ]     [ 3 ]        labels in a separate tray, stacked fractions
[ 4 ]     [ 2 ]     [ 6 ]

0                1/2                 1
|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
  [selected fraction]  [Move left] [Move right]
[Read the steps]   [Help]                     [Check]
```

Illustrative only: the actual layout must avoid putting the tray over line coordinates, use 44×44 CSS-pixel minimum controls and reflow without horizontal clipping at narrow widths/enlarged type. Selection and controls need visible text, focus, and state beyond color. Suppress nonessential motion for reduced motion.

Select a tray label, then drag it onto the line, tap a line location, or use movement controls. A first movement chooses an offered endpoint explicitly, announces it, and never silently places a token. A selected placed label can be moved with the same paths. Live pointer feedback identifies the nearest offered candidate; on release inside the valid drop region commit that candidate, with a deterministic lower-position tie break. Pointer cancellation, lost capture, or invalid/outside drop restores that member's previous location, including unplaced. No mathematical correctness is inferred from pixels; geometry only selects a prevalidated candidate ID. A tap is an explicit placement. Keyboard selection and movement must produce the same draft as pointer/touch, with focus retained on the selected member/control and announced position. Do not treat emulated touch as physical-device proof.

At an exact shared position, draw **one point/anchor** and separately selectable labels in vertical rows above that point, each joined by a thin, non-interactive tether to the same anchor. At a shared anchor, place the first authored member in the lower row and the second in the row directly above it; reserve a third row for a future three-way case. Their display boxes may move, never their mathematical coordinates. Select each label by its own named control, with stable authored member order for keyboard focus, including co-located members; taps hit the labels or their separate member controls, not an ambiguous shared point. At narrow widths keep these vertical rows, reserve their height before placement, and clamp label boxes within the line container while drawing each tether back to the unchanged anchor. A separate stable member list remains available if a clamped box collides with another anchor; use that list to select either member rather than covering a control. Linear access presents each member in that stable order with its unplaced/position status and the same offered steps; selecting a member and a step changes only that member. Announce co-location neutrally (“two labels at this point”), not as an answer verdict. Distinct original forms and stable IDs remain distinct even when their exact values agree.

**Journey:** initially all unplaced. Check remains available: for incomplete placement, identify the unplaced labels and ask the learner to place them, preserving every placement; it does not grade the placed subset. With all placed, Check evaluates the full collection. On a wrong Check, associate concise “check this placement” feedback with each incorrect member and “placed correctly” with correct members; do not reveal target coordinates or infer misconceptions. Keep *all* placements, including correct ones, editable; clear stale member feedback when that member changes and mark the collection as needing another Check. One new Check reassesses the entire current map. On acceptance, show the same exact anchors and original labels, a concise same-point/equivalence statement when applicable, and direct **Next problem**; final item offers **Back to start**. No individual auto-acceptance, auto-advance, or empty Continue/Finish. Focus goes to the feedback summary on Check, with linked member feedback, then remains predictable on revision and moves to the new episode on Next.

Help is optional and says to consider the size of parts and compare positions, without naming answers; record that help was requested and its kind even if closed. More/Less are explicit support profiles, never performance adaptation: More may show partition marks and the same neutral step descriptions; Less may reduce decorative marks, but both retain the identical offered lattice and non-drag/linear choices. Switching must not change placements or introduce new right answers. Support and help policy belongs to instructional state, not a renderer. Record assistance as provenance; a correct helped answer is not evidence of unassisted mastery.


Illustrative partial placement and shared-anchor layout (A, B, C identify the original stacked fraction controls; vertical lanes are not extra number-line coordinates):

```text
Members: [A: 3/4 — unplaced] [B: 1/2 — at 4 of 8] [C: 3/6 — at 4 of 8]
Tray:    [A: 3/4]                              Select A, B, or C above
                             [C: 3/6]          upper row: selectable label
                                  |
                             [B: 1/2]          lower row: selectable label
                                  |             both tethers end at the same point
0-----------------------------●------------------------------1
                              4 of 8
Selected: B     [Move left] [Move right]  [Read the steps]  [Check]
```

The member list persists in authored A, B, C order whether unplaced or placed, with each name, current status and selection state; the separate tray holds only unplaced labels. Selecting B or C from the list or its own visible label and then using a line tap, drag, movement button or linear choice repositions only that member. Tab order is the stable member list, then movement/linear controls and Check; the duplicated visual labels provide pointer targets without extra tab stops. Keyboard and linear users select through the list, and receive each member's status and updates in that order. When all three draft labels occupy one point, show A, B and C in the lower, middle and upper rows in authored order, with three distinct tethers ending at the *same* anchor; each remains selectable via the list and its visible label. Wrong draft collisions are permitted. If labels from neighboring anchors would overlap at narrow widths, keep the reserved rows and exact anchors, use separated clamped boxes where feasible, and rely on the always-visible member list for unambiguous selection; never stack one interactive target over another. A rendered implementation must verify tether legibility and collision behavior; this paper sketch does not prove it.
## Compact authoring and exact capability

Proposed record: `{ id, revision, purpose: 'same-location-and-order', lattice: 8, members: [{ id: 'a', form: { numerator: '3', denominator: '4' } }, ...] }`, with three stable unique member IDs, original nonnegative wire forms and an explicit authored input order. Store no answer positions, sorted order or equivalence flags. At admission, derive exact values with `createFraction`, `compareFractions`/`equalFractions`; derive offered positions as exact `i/lattice` for `i=0..lattice`, matching each source value exactly to one candidate. Equivalence compares values, while member identity stays independent. Validate positive denominator, [0,1], unique IDs, three members, and an exact match on the common lattice. Require at least two distinct values and at least one unused offered point, so all-equivalent or answer-revealing three-point menus are deferred. Curate unsorted input order and retain wrong choices; never derive the offered set solely from answers.

Use one fixed lattice per item from **{4, 6, 8}**, a conservative subset of Plan 39's existing adopted {2, 3, 4, 6, 8} partition support; 0 through b are offered. Raw source denominators ≤8; a source denominator need not equal or divide b if its *value* matches a lattice point (e.g. 3/6 on an eighths line). An irreducible third on an eighths line fails this proposed admission even though mathematically valid; 1/10 exceeds the proposed source bound. This is a new collection capability policy, not a claim the unary Plan 37 validator accepts these records. Existing sparse mode has four authored candidates and unary assumptions; do not silently reuse it as a collection solution. Larger denominators, [0,2], mixed/improper values and random activation need separate work. Zero and one are permitted only as exact boundary values in this proposed slice.

The executable report-local `exact-examples.mjs` uses the existing exact APIs, comparing every member to every lattice candidate. ## State, lifecycle and feasibility

A new task-specific interaction state should own `{memberId -> candidateId | null}`, selected member ID, evaluation/feedback revision and assistance history. Establish it as authoritative above renderers; no accepted positions until a complete aggregate Check succeeds. Pointer and direct controls issue raw select/move/place/cancel intents; location IDs must belong to the active exact offered set and member IDs to the active collection. Check submits a complete snapshot; interaction validates identity/membership and exact equality for each member, not DOM coordinates or renderer-produced correctness. The fixed schedule is `place-collection → resolve`; partial and wrong Check stay at placement. On acceptance atomically establish the full accepted map and provenance. Historical definitions and replay envelopes must not be reinterpreted; a separately reviewed versioned collection identity/replay contract must serialize meaningful raw intents, help and aggregate checks and reproduce partial, wrong and accepted states.

The app coordinates access-view, help disclosure and focus, while instructional state owns placements, selection, support and assistance. Ephemeral pointer hover/preview may remain renderer-local; durable placements must survive view switch, help open/close, resize and renderer remount. Focus is restored to the selected member or an appropriate equivalent control after remount; if absent use the task heading. Invalidation identity includes task/record and revisions, member IDs/forms, lattice/offered-set revision and relevant support policy, not just visual geometry. Same episode resize/access switch preserves draft; explicit restart creates fresh empty map, selection, feedback and help/provenance; Next creates a fresh identity/state; final return ends the session, and a later start is fresh. Failed navigation must leave the current accepted result intact. Replay obligations include partial placements, co-location, wrong revision, help, support and acceptance; no promise of persisted browser sessions without a separately specified persistence contract.

`src/content/plan37-bar-to-line-bridge-task.js` derives exact offered scale; `src/interaction/scene.js` projects candidates; `src/interaction/episode.js` currently checks **one** offered wire fraction using `equalFractions`; `src/render/number-line-locate.js` owns one preview and candidate controls, and `src/app/app.js` stores one opaque draft and help/focus timing. This makes reuse plausible, not a ready-made collection implementation. A bounded collection-specific state/scene and adapted line control are needed; do not force a map into the unary preview handle or build a general representation engine. Plan 43's accepted direct progression informs Next/final return; actual integration and replay compatibility need new source evidence. Plans 45 and 49 are owner-accepted at the reviewed baseline; their comparison assistance policy is task-specific and does not bar this different multi-label task.

Visual/linear switching is an **access-path change of the same number line**, not a bar/line representation change. This task does not prove Roadmap 35 representation switching. Defer an actual bar/line switch: a meaningful later bridge should ask the learner to connect an amount to a line location and preserve member identity, placements, help and established reasoning across the change; inserting a switch here would add an unrelated task.

## Alternative disposition

The alternative of three simultaneous independent location selectors would preserve member identity and one Check, but would repeat a potentially long set of step choices three times and weaken the shared-line action in a narrow layout. Prefer selecting one member and choosing its location, while retaining per-member status and direct non-drag controls. This is a paper judgment; a rendered review may falsify it if selection, tethers or focus order are confusing or crowded. Neither design has been evaluated with learners or assistive technology.

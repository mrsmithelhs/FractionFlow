## Recommended publication approach

1. Decide the fixed-set tradeoff and review the staged tasks as teaching material. Today learners traverse the active set from its beginning to exhaustion. Keeping old IDs as a prefix can make the first new item arrive late and can make a bank larger than its count of distinct exact pairs. In particular, Plan 35 would have 36 entries and Plan 51 would have 32 if all old IDs and staged records were appended. The planned short-draw work is not current behavior.
2. Start with a bounded versioned pilot, preferably Plan 40, whose existing v1/v2 association and replay path provides a precedent. Preserve all existing definitions, fixture identities, sets and meanings; add a new ordered revision, not a silent rewrite. Verify historical and new replay, first newly reached item, fixed Next and exhaustion, both views, narrow/enlarged text, and unchanged authored sessions and scheduler selections.
3. Design the other seams by family: Plan 35 has legacy and recipe associations; Plans 74/78/82 and 50/51/59 have fixed-v1 historical resolvers; Plan 45 has a fixed-12 batch contract and Plan 49 help; Plan 36 has the fixed-eight materializer as well as array-derived live membership. These are review topics, not permission for broad modernization. Review choice/cue balance, results, and ordering before any activation.
4. Bring each slice back for integrated technical and owner review. The final first-new-item and end-of-set evidence cannot be gathered through current production membership for these staged candidates. Do not count report-only admission as learner reach, and do not alter scheduling to hide the fixed-set tradeoff.

## Recommendation and choice

Offer a separate short practice goal, **How far apart are these amounts?** Present the two original fractions as first and second. Ask for one relation `<`, `=`, or `>` with full spoken labels and Check; that choice establishes which amount is larger without a second equivalent “which is greater?” question. For unequal amounts, keep the accepted relation as context and ask for a fraction giving the distance. The learner can compute larger minus smaller, choosing the order using the relation. This connects comparison to subtraction as *distance between amounts*. It does not remove the second amount from a starting collection: Plan 36's adopted takeaway has a different meaning and grouped removal action.

Use **aligned bars** only as optional distance help and after acceptance. Begin the distance question symbolically, with no paired model or gap readout. On requested model help show honest, equally sized wholes, common partitions, and the two labeled amounts; the learner can count the difference. This is assisted model reading and may reveal the distance, even without a numeric gap label. Record the help; do not call the ensuing answer independent computation. A non-revealing text cue is also available: “Use the greater amount minus the smaller amount. The parts have the same size.” No disguised, inaccurate, or selectively hidden bars. After acceptance, keep the learner's accepted equivalent fraction and show the paired bars as verification, with no separate reflection or Finish.

The rival measured-line interval is a legitimate *reading length* task, not independent symbolic computation. Plan 39 established amount-to-coordinate on [0,1], not subtraction interval length; its point/connector does not establish a ready distance control. A line makes endpoint versus length and whole-unit scale additional first-slice design obligations. Bars align with accepted comparison verification and the initial common-unit content; this is a design judgment, not evidence that bars teach better. Return to the rival if source inspection or owner review finds bar help unhelpful or misleading.

## Exact content and capacity

`evidence/exact-examples.mjs` uses current `createFraction`, `compareFractions`, `equalFractions`, `leastCommonDenominator`, `convertToDenominator`, `subtractFractions` and `simplifyFraction`; its generated JSON records eight ordered cases including swaps, equivalent equality and unlike units. For `3/5` versus `5/9`, exact common units are 27/45 and 25/45 and distance 2/45; 45 parts are a deliberate capacity warning, not authorization to render that partition. Swaps preserve unsigned distance while computational order changes. First/second raw forms remain separate from normalized identity and computational order; select larger using exact comparison, then subtract. Core subtraction rejects negative results. Exact API results demonstrate arithmetic for these cases, not eligibility, UI or teaching efficacy.

Recommend an initial fixed four-record batch from evidence rows: `2/8,6/8`; its swap; `5/6,3/6`; `5/6,2/6`. Positive proper operands, same denominator 6 or 8, no mixed/improper or random content. Denominator is the count of equal parts in **one whole**, not the number of shaded parts or available graphic width. Each bar must have the same whole width and subdivision scale even at resize. Readable common partition and content eligibility must be validated upstream; the existing general bar ceiling (decision log DECISION-008) is not by itself proof of readability for this new scene. A unit interval's length would be endpoint difference, not endpoint coordinate; a domain width is not necessarily one whole. Equality and unlike-denominator records are excluded from the first four but exact-audited above; require a separate admitted equality branch test and deliberate expansion decision before enabling either. No larger-denominator policy, multi-whole, random selection, mastery or persistence implied.

# Subtraction Prototype Observation Guide

Use this short guide while a learner tries both ways to show the same fraction
subtraction problems. Watch what the learner does before offering help. These
observations are design evidence, not an efficacy claim or formal research.

## Before a session

- Get appropriate adult permission before observing a child.
- Use the four built-in synthetic examples. Do not enter names or other personal
  information.
- Alternate which representation is tried first when practical. Record the
  order and which examples the learner has already seen; the second attempt may
  benefit from prior exposure.
- High-school feedback can reveal interaction issues. It is not target-age
  child-usability evidence.

## Behaviors to notice

1. Where does the learner pause or reread before acting?
2. Can the learner tell what the control will do before pressing it?
3. After acting, what does the learner say the crossed parts or bracket mean?
4. What does the learner say the fraction they entered counts? When do they
   change an answer, request help, or stop?

Record what happened in plain language: the representation, example, action,
visible response, and any help or retry. A hesitation is a prompt to look closer,
not a verdict by itself. Repeatedly confusing an action or being unable to
describe its meaning is useful evidence for the next design decision.

## Handling notes

Collect only what is needed to understand the interaction. Retained notes must
be de-identified and limited to observed behavior, design impact, and follow-up
questions. Do not put names, contact details, identifiable screenshots,
recordings, student work, or other learner artifacts in the public repository,
issue tracker, fixtures, or deployment. If permission, safe handling, or
de-identification cannot be established, do not retain the observation or
artifact; record the design question without identifying material.

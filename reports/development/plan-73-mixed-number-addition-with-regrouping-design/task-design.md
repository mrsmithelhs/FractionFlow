# Plan 73 — Mixed-number addition with regrouping: design comparison

**Recommendation for owner review, not an adopted design or implementation.** On source baseline `a0bae8d1d93509786347a02fc7b01232d378d894`, recommend the single final response as the first slice, with optional non-answering unit help. The exchange is a plausible later comparison, but its extra required step earns its cost only if the product needs evidence of the learner's explicit grouping decision, or learner observation shows that making it improves understanding enough to justify the burden. Neither has been established here. Both designs require the learner to determine the final counts; only the second additionally observes a proposed grouping. No learner or rendered evaluation of either proposal was performed.

## Common task and boundary

First slice: positive mixed operands with positive proper fractional parts, a shared denominator within each item, and fractional parts totaling at least one but less than two wholes. Each item therefore permits exactly one new whole. Preserve the source denominator, without requiring simplification. Two proposed first-batch items, ordered zero then nonzero, are the exact packet examples; the tiny batch is a design starting point, not a sufficient practice dose or efficacy claim. A separately approved implementation must define authored identities, denominator and count limits, and admission.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zero | 1 3/8 + 2 5/8 | 3 wholes and 8 eighths | 4 wholes and 0 eighths; display **4** |
| Nonzero | 1 5/8 + 2 6/8 | 3 wholes and 11 eighths | 4 wholes and 3 eighths; display **4 3/8** |

The count pairs above describe reasoning, not prefilled initial answers. [Exact evidence](exact-evidence.mjs), run with `node reports/development/plan-73-mixed-number-addition-with-regrouping-design/exact-evidence.mjs`, uses `addMixedNumbers`, exact fraction addition/comparison, and equality of ungrouped and grouped values. It checks both examples, an unequal witness for each, and `1 3/8 + 2 4/8 = 3 7/8`, whose seven pieces are below one whole and must never offer an exchange. It checks mathematical value and the boundary, not UI behavior or pedagogy.

Both candidates initially show the same original operands as grouped stacked mixed notation and equal-scale models, with coherent accessible names. They provide the same optional unit help: an unfilled eighths whole and “How many eighths make one whole? Look at the whole units and the eighth pieces in both amounts.” It supplies neither pooled nor final counts. More/Less should truthfully offer the same aid unless a separately designed difference exists. Keyboard and linear access are available in either setting. The learner can close help and retain the draft; help use belongs to the attempt and survives retry and same-attempt reconstruction.

## Candidate A: one final response

Prompt: “Add the amounts. Enter the whole units and remaining eighth pieces in the sum.” Two initially blank fields, **Whole units in the sum** and **Remaining eighth pieces**, have a fixed `/ 8` suffix for the second, whose accessible name includes the unit. One Check evaluates the pair. A blank means not entered, never zero. A learner must explicitly enter `0` for a zero remainder; accepting whole-only **4** refers to the app's displayed result after `4`, `0` are checked, not to treating a blank field as zero. Nonzero acceptance displays app-supplied grouped stacked **4 3/8**, preserving eighths, with attribution that the learner supplied the counts and the app formatted the numeral.

| Journey | Learner action and check | What feedback can show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero | From `1 3/8 + 2 5/8`, learner determines `4` wholes and `0` eighths; Check accepts. | The accepted settled model shows the same 3 original whole units plus eight original eighth pieces arranged into a fourth equal-scale whole, with 0 eighths left. State “8 eighths make 1 whole; 4 wholes, 0 eighths.” Display 4. |
| Nonzero | From `1 5/8 + 2 6/8`, learner determines `4` and `3`; Check accepts. | The accepted model groups eight of the eleven original pieces into one whole and retains three pieces: “8 eighths make 1 whole; 4 wholes, 3 eighths.” Display 4 3/8. |

Feedback after acceptance can illustrate an equivalence; it cannot establish that the learner used it. The learner may have counted, used an algorithm, guessed, or had outside help. The app observes entries, checks, and recorded help, not strategy or independent understanding. Before acceptance do not expose pooled totals, grouped answer, hidden solution model, or answer-bearing accessibility labels.

## Candidate B: one brief learner-controlled exchange

Show the same original whole units and source pieces, without prefilled pooled or final counts. Ask “How many eighth pieces make one whole?” The learner enters a count in an initially blank **Eighth pieces to exchange** field and activates **Make a whole**. This is the one additional mathematical decision: the learner chooses the number of source pieces equal to one whole. The app checks the count and that enough source pieces exist; blank, malformed, or an incorrect count receives neutral, actionable guidance, retaining the entry for revision. It does not supply the count. Below one whole, the exchange is unavailable; this boundary is outside the proposed regrouping batch. Optional help remains the same unfilled unit as in A.

On a valid exchange, the app groups that many existing source pieces into one equal-scale whole and leaves all other original pieces visible; it does not create or discard quantity. The learner does not select individual pieces: the app chooses a deterministic grouping of interchangeable source pieces and labels their origins, without claiming the learner chose which pieces. The settled view reveals the added whole and the remaining pieces, making final counts countable and potentially apparent. The learner then uses the same two initially blank final fields and Check as A; the learner accounts for the remaining pieces there, including explicit `0`. The model supports the final answer, so the final Check is not independent evidence of calculating the total. Before final acceptance, an Undo exchange control returns to the original arrangement, preserves the exchange entry and final draft, and clears stale feedback; the learner can revise and activate again. A new valid exchange invalidates prior final feedback. Same-attempt replay preserves entry, exchange, undo, final edits, checks and help; a fresh attempt resets them.

| Journey | Learner decision and final response | Quantity shown after exchange |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zero | From `1 3/8 + 2 5/8`, enter `8` and activate Make a whole; enter final `4`, `0` and Check. | The 3 original wholes remain; all 8 source eighths form one equal-scale whole, leaving 0 eighths. The accepted numeral is 4. |
| Nonzero | From `1 5/8 + 2 6/8`, enter `8` and activate Make a whole; enter final `4`, `3` and Check. | The 3 original wholes remain; 8 of the 11 source eighths form one equal-scale whole and 3 source eighths remain. The accepted numeral is 4 3/8. |

Compared with A, this adds one count entry and action, as well as an intermediate state and an undo path. It can observe the submitted unit count and final response, with recorded help and retries; it cannot establish that the learner chose the individual pieces, independently computed the remainder, understood equivalence, or can transfer it. The settled model may make the final response easier by revealing countable quantities. This leaner B is a credible way to make equivalence visible before the final response, but its extra action has not been shown to earn its cost. Recommend A for the first slice pending learner observation or a specific need to observe the unit-count decision; B is a candidate for a later bounded comparison, not a measured improvement.

## Checking and recovery for both

The prospective checker first handles missing, malformed and out-of-range inputs with neutral field-specific guidance; it does not score or replace drafts. For valid counts, compare the exact mixed value before judging organization. If unequal, say “Check the amount and try again.” If equal but the piece count is at least the denominator, say “Your amount is the same. Group enough pieces to make a whole, then check again.” For example `3` and `8` in the zero case and `3` and `11` in the nonzero case have the correct value but are ungrouped. If admitted, both must reach organization guidance, not an unequal-sum error. The future field bounds must admit these witnesses or explicitly explain a narrower range before value classification; a design that promises this feedback cannot hide them behind a limit of eight. `4` and `1` for the zero case is unequal. Editing either final field clears stale feedback and preserves the other; Check uses the current revision. For B, exchange validation retains the learner's count on retry; final validation retains both final entries. Avoid giving final counts in help or error messages.

## Equivalent visual and linear paths

All wholes use the same width and unit scale. In A's accepted visualization, original pieces are traceable as a group of eight plus any remainder; exchange does not create or delete pieces or alter the unit. A textual settled summary gives all quantities and the same equivalence. In B the visual path shows the original source pieces and the same exchange count field and Make a whole control as the linear path. The linear path names both original amounts and their pieces, exposes the same editable count and action, and describes the settled grouping and remaining pieces. Neither requires dragging, color perception, or selecting pieces individually. Both paths offer the same learner decision and reveal the same quantities; switching preserves draft, exchange and final-response state. The app's deterministic grouping is described as app-supplied. Optional motion may illustrate the checked exchange; reduced motion or skipping it immediately shows the identical settled state. Announce one concise feedback target without duplicate spoken fragments; maintain focus on edit, return focus from Help, and offer clear keyboard order. Actual assistive-technology speech needs later testing.

Stack each mixed numeral as a single grouped object with one accessible name, retaining source denominator. Label originals, exchanged pieces, remaining pieces, original wholes and the new whole so that color and spatial position are not the only distinctions. A complete fallback summary must include original whole count, total source-piece count, exchanged count, new whole count, remaining piece count, and resulting total whole count when those states are established; distinguish unknown or not entered from zero. The settled summary may expose final counts; before exchange, do not disclose computed pooled or final totals through hidden labels. Never substitute a partial summary for an unreadable graphic.

These are prior browser observations, not proof of this design. Eleven pieces, a new whole, the exchange field and intermediate summaries can increase height and create cramped targets or overflow. A complete count summary must preserve the same exchange decision and settled information as the visual model.

A future implementation must capture its own rendered checks at 360 × 740, normal and 200% root text, for both cases: initial, intermediate exchange if B is chosen, partial/zero, retry and help, and acceptance. Check full-width overflow, readable fixed suffix and stacked numerals, stable equal unit scale, complete summaries, exchange controls, focus/caret, path switching, keyboard operation and optional/reduced motion. Include a non-drag path. CSS root scaling is not actual browser zoom; physical devices, real assistive-technology speech and learner understanding remain separate checks.

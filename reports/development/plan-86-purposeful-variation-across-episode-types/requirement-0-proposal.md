## Inventory and choice

Changing a numerical record alone changes the numbers; selecting visual versus linear or requesting help changes support/representation; neither by itself changes the mathematical decision. Plan 35 addition and Plan 40 subtraction currently register construct, inspect/keep/repair and identify-side-then-construct recipes (`src/content/data/plan35-addition-recipe-records.js`, `src/content/data/plan40-reusable-subtraction-recipes-records.js`). Construction elicits equivalent-form production for both operands; inspection asks whether a supplied form preserves the amount and requires repair when it does not; identification asks which one operand needs renaming when the other already has the target unit. Each serves amount preservation and a subsequent operation, but repeated construction can feel procedural. Existing inspect and identify tasks are already reachable on other records; the proposals below are **new associations for particular existing numbers**, not new recipe inventions. An app-supplied candidate is not learner work.

A third family, Plan 45 comparison, asks the learner to choose `<`, `=` or `>` and check; its schedule is compare → resolve, with optional reasoning/model help (`src/content/plan45-symbolic-fraction-comparison-task.js`, `src/interaction/beat-schedule.js`). It practices ordering rather than performing an operation, and offers an already short decision. Different support or a new pair does not itself create a new task. We do not recommend adding a second decision to that episode in this pilot. This is a source-based design judgment, not evidence of boredom or learning outcomes.

**Recommend two** review-only alternatives, one for unlike-denominator addition and one for unlike-denominator subtraction. They reuse the existing inspect pattern, but attach it to the same numerical fixtures currently assigned construction. Each replaces one construction with a keep judgment and the other with a repair judgment; it does not add an extra step or remove the final arithmetic. This tests whether diagnosis is useful without presenting the app's supplied work as the child's. The proposed examples reverse which side is wrong so that a fixed side is not a reliable shortcut. They are two task routes through two existing numerical problems, not two new numerical problems.

Before every judgment, visual mode presents the original and candidate notation with a stable whole and only non-revealing representation; linear mode states the same original, candidate, target and choices in reading order, including numerator and denominator in accessible names. Optional local help in either mode explains that equal amounts must cover the same part of the whole and offers the same available conversion guidance and agency; any answer-revealing aid must be requested, identified as help, and recorded as assistance rather than learner production. Help does not choose Keep or submit a response. Preserve an editable wrong answer, feedback and help when changing view; identify focus for errors, controls, and advancement in both modes. Admission must resolve the exact fixture, authoring revision, operation, source forms and reviewed profile; require a positive canonical integer target that is a common denominator, both operands needing conversion, current presentation eligibility and a proper positive result within each existing family contract. Validate each candidate's target denominator, canonical nonnegative numerator and bounds; exact rational equality must show **exactly one** equivalent candidate and one non-equivalent candidate. Keep is accepted iff the candidate equals its source; repair is accepted iff the submitted numerator over the target equals its source, and the operation is accepted under the existing family's validation policy. Invalid, empty or incorrect responses cannot advance. Preserve source, candidate, accepted input and honest constructed/kept/repaired/supplied provenance separately; the existing classifier also distinguishes an unchanged edit from a repair.

## Rejected alternative, ownership and evidence

Reject adding “Which operand needs renaming?” before these two-operand construct tasks: both operands need conversion, so a one-side choice has no valid answer; extending it to “both” would add a question rather than replace work. Existing identify-side records with exactly one conversion remain useful, but are already reachable and are not a new pilot here. Also do not infer that addition/subtraction inspection automatically transfers to comparison or that this is engaging.

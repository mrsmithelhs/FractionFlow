# Plan 54 — Whole-plus-fraction construction design

2026-11-02. Recommendation for owner disposition, not adoption, an implementation or Phase 4 exit. Scope approved in `scope-approval.md`; source baseline after approval `7487fa0414b5baf538bc2d3b15d0d8dc3c43c2c7`. Source claims below refer to this baseline. Earlier Plan 53 and Plan 48 reports are evidence at their own pinned baselines. Sketches are unmeasured. No learner, physical-device or assistive-technology outcome is established.

## Recommendation and comparison

Offer a **separate short task**: supply an improper fraction, ask the learner to choose how many whole units and how many remaining source-denominator pieces make that amount, and show their tentative choices in a model. Check once; allow revision and optional help; after acceptance show the app-supplied mixed numeral beside the accepted construction and offer Next. The learner determines both quantities and thereby establishes the grouping; the app supplies the original improper fraction and later notation. This is not a mixed-number entry or independent notation-conversion test, nor an automatic sequel to a location task. Use proper and whole-only contrasts sparingly in an explicitly authored pilot. This is a design judgment, not evidence of superior teaching.

| Comparison | Manipulate whole and fractional units | Enter counts with model response (recommended) |
|---|---|---|
| Learner decision | Select and group units to match supplied amount; decide when a group is a whole. | Determine whole count and leftover source units; inspect an editable model that reflects those choices. |
| Supplied / wrong response | Source fraction, same-size empty unit spaces and source unit; wrong groups reveal missing/excess units or unorganized grouping. | Same source and unit reference; wrong counts reveal wrong amount, and an improper remainder reveals a valid but unorganized amount. |
| Recovery and help | Moving selected groups can be direct, but accidental placement, dragging, object selection and repeated piece assembly require a robust equivalent. Help can emphasize how many source pieces make one whole. | Two initially blank count controls; edit either in one action, with model reflecting draft only. Help states how many pieces make a whole without giving how many wholes or leftover pieces. |
| Access and seams | Requires selected-object semantics, grouping boundaries, keyboard selection/move/cancel, touch targets and recovery; may better externalize grouping but increases interaction and focus burden. | Native labeled numeric inputs or equivalent select controls plus static labeled model; keyboard/linear access makes the same count decisions, though visual inspection and linear textual feedback give different cues. Requires response owner, projected draft and withholding, not an object-drag system. |

Neither accepted Plan 53 nor Plan 48 compares learning from these alternatives. A count model risks learners calculating symbolically without attending to grouping; a manipulative could make grouping more concrete but cannot be assumed better or more accessible. The count approach is the smallest testable responsibility with direct correction and avoids compulsory piece-by-piece assembly. Its proposed model must actually map counts to same-size units and make the grouping visible; a mere decorative answer echo would falsify the rationale. Existing evidence supports a bounded choice, not a comparative teaching claim; no consequential unresolved question needs an external search to make this limited recommendation. If owner needs evidence of comparative teaching effectiveness, first specify population, tasks and transfer, then use the approved at-most-three-source targeted search.

## Mathematical, form and model contract

Recommend the following **six-record first batch**, within 0 ≤ n/d ≤ 2 and d in {2,4,8}; this is an owner choice, not an admitted production set. **3/4** contrasts a proper amount and explicit zero wholes; **4/4** exercises the interior whole and zero remainder; **5/4** establishes one whole plus one piece; **6/4** preserves an unreduced remainder and tests valid-but-unorganized drafts; **3/2** presents the same value in a different supplied unit; **8/4** exercises the upper boundary and two whole units. The eight API examples in `evidence/exact-examples.json` support arithmetic only and do not admit a batch. Eighths are deferred from this first recommendation: Plan 53 accepted them, but its different model does not establish their construction usability. Adding 10/8 requires owner choice and actual capability checks. Do not widen the existing Plan 53 task.

For this first task, the remainder control counts **source-denominator pieces**, so the learner cannot change denominator within this interaction. No simplification is required: 6/4 asks for 1 whole and 2 fourths; 3/2 asks for 1 whole and 1 half. They are numerically equal, retain distinct supplied forms, and both are valid. A future free-denominator interface could accept 1 and 1/2 for 6/4 if exactly equivalent and visibly communicates the conversion; do not silently relabel or force simplification. Raw source strings, draft counts, unit denominator, and accepted form are kept separately from exact value.

Both fields start blank, distinct from explicit zero; validate nonnegative integer input and bound fields to 0–2 wholes and 0–2d remaining pieces so a valid-but-unorganized 0 wholes and 5 fourths can be represented. Empty/malformed/out-of-range is incomplete, not wrong arithmetic. Once complete, use `createMixedNumber` / `mixedToImproper` and `equalFractions` for exact comparison, then independently require remainder count < d. Classification order: invalid/incomplete; unequal amount (retry); equal amount with remainder ≥ d (neutral organization request, preserving input); equal amount with remainder < d (accept). For 0 wholes and 5 fourths, show neutral feedback “That is” followed by the original stacked 5 over 4, then “Can you make a whole from the fourths?” The accessible name reads the fraction coherently. Never say the arithmetic is wrong. For a whole-only result accept explicit zero remainder and display the whole alone; proper-only result accepts zero wholes and a proper remainder and displays the fraction alone; zero accepts both zero and displays 0. Core transient mixed forms are allowed to have an improper remainder (`src/math/mixed-number.js`); task organization is an additional rule. Never auto-normalize the draft before the learner acts.

A blank field means unknown, never zero: show “Whole units: not entered” or “Remaining fourths: not entered” for that region, and render only quantities explicitly entered in the other region. Explicit zero is shown as zero and no filled pieces. For every permitted complete or partial draft, use two separate regions: up to two whole rows for the entered whole count, and up to two additional source-piece rows for the entered remainder count, each row one identical physical whole wide, partitioned into d equal pieces. Fill the latter rows in order up to d pieces each; label them “Remaining fourths” even when the count is at least d. For example, 2 wholes and 8 fourths displays two filled whole rows and two filled fourths rows, a total capacity of four same-scale wholes, without converting either region into the other. Stack all four rows vertically at narrow widths, keeping the same unit width and piece scale. If even this reflow cannot fit readably, replace the entire graphical draft with an exact text summary of the learner’s entered counts, for example “2 whole units; 8 remaining fourths,” and retain the editable controls; an unknown field is named “not entered.” Do not shrink individual units, silently truncate pieces, or normalize the draft. This fallback reports the learner’s count, not a computed answer. A remainder count ≥ d is labeled as source pieces, never as a mixed numeral. The model pictures only the draft, not an independent solution or prefilled completed grouping. A whole is d source pieces, and a remainder piece is 1/d of that same whole. Counting pieces, deciding which d constitute a whole, and writing the mixed numeral are distinct acts. No line is needed during construction: the unit model supports thinking; after acceptance its existing accepted grouping remains visible as verification, alongside the notation. A later optional line is outside this slice, not an assumed representation switch. DECISION-032 establishes equal-scale stacks; Plan 48 explains why stack and cumulative line endpoints should not be falsely connected (`../plan-48-whole-boundary-bridge-and-mixed-number-readiness/readiness-and-design.md`).

## Journeys and copy

**5/4; wrong, help, revise.** Instruction “Show” followed by stacked 5 over 4, then “as wholes and fourths.” Both counts blank; no model answer. Learner enters 1 whole and 2 fourths, sees their own full row plus two quarter pieces, presses Check. Retry: “Check the amount and try again.” Draft stays. They request Help: “How many fourths make one whole?” with an unfilled four-part reference, not a target count. They change remainder to 1 and Check. The model and supplied stacked 5 over 4 remain, and the app presents one grouped mixed numeral: whole 1 next to stacked 1 over 4. “That’s right.” is the only acknowledgment; no additional equality sentence is needed. Next is immediately available. The learner has established 1 whole and 1 fourth; the app has supplied the conventional mixed notation.

**6/4; equivalent but unorganized.** Learner enters 0 wholes and 6 fourths, checks, and sees “That is” followed by the original stacked 6 over 4, then “Can you make a whole from the fourths?” The six pieces remain; no red incorrect treatment. Help asks “How many fourths make one whole?” Learner revises to 1 whole and 2 fourths; Check accepts, then the app shows the mixed numeral 1 with stacked 2/4, not forced 1/2. Shared Next, or Back on the final item, is direct. A wrong whole-only draft such as 2 wholes and 0 fourths receives amount-retry, not organization feedback.

**State and access.** Controls have explicit labels “Whole units” and “Remaining fourths” (denominator varies); blank controls expose no preselection. A draft updates only the explicitly entered region of the model; a blank field remains visibly unknown, and the draft remains tentative. Check without both counts: “Enter both amounts.” Revise clears obsolete feedback but preserves both values and assistance history. Accepted state locks controls, retains raw values and model, reveals app notation and direct progression. Use Plan 52 success/retry/neutral-attention semantics and one associated feedback announcement on Check; no duplicate live regions or automatic focus hopping. Optional Help opens locally, preserves draft, offers an equal-unit reference and records request; closing does not erase assistance. More and Less use the same useful reference and counts in this pilot, not a nominal toggle with fake differences. View changes and resize preserve draft, checked feedback, help visibility and assistance; reset creates a fresh blank attempt, closes help and invalidates stale callbacks. Replay must reconstruct source identity, entered raw counts, check/revise/help intents, assistance, acceptance and final form. A canceled edit does not commit. Reduced motion displays static changes.

Use ordinary keyboard-operable inputs, associated labels, clear focus, bounded correction and an explicitly labeled textual draft summary in linear view; do not require dragging. Visual piece highlights identify which counts are selected, boundaries identify separate whole and remainder regions, and controls remain usable at touch spacing. Linear view should not prestate a correct answer; its summary describes only the learner's choices. Different modalities can offer different cues; equivalence of mathematical decisions is a design goal requiring testing, not a claim of identical experience. Future implementation must verify focus, announcements, physical touch and real assistive technology separately.

## Mixed typography

Create one grouped mixed-numeral component: whole numeral adjacent to an intrinsic-width vertically stacked fraction, baseline and spacing readable as one number, one accessible name such as “one and two fourths.” Reuse Plan 31's structured stacked fraction and its coherent spoken-name strategy (`../plan-31-shared-fraction-typography/progress.md` and `mechanism-review.md`); a new wrapper and one complete spoken name are needed for the whole-plus-fraction group. Hide duplicate visual fragments from the accessibility tree only when the complete equivalent name exists. Do not use ambiguous inline `1 2/4` as the visual contract or present a plus sign as if it were mixed notation. For 4/4 show `1`; for 3/4 show stacked 3 over 4; for 6/4 show whole 1 adjacent to stacked 2 over 4; a later wider-range example uses whole 12 adjacent to stacked 11 over 16. Keep each group unbroken, let surrounding prose wrap and enlarge, avoid clipping or horizontal overflow; if a group cannot fit, reflow its surrounding line, not split its numerals. These are unmeasured design requirements, not evidence that arbitrary two-digit values fit the initial task.

## Readiness and owner choices

Plan 53 owner acceptance covers the location task, not learner-owned grouping or learner outcomes (`../plan-53-two-whole-number-line-location/owner-acceptance.md`). Plan 48's readiness review identifies partial rather than general evidence of representation continuity and operation meaning. Roadmap §§35–37 and 91 still require a disposition on same-object continuity through representation switches, honest geometry, operation movement on the line, stable uncluttered practice, and the evidence for mixed-number readiness; OQ-24 asks for a broader strategy. This proposed task could be tried as a foundation after owner adoption and a separate source gate. It does not itself close OQ-24, establish Phase 4 exit, or demonstrate readiness for mixed arithmetic.

Later dependencies, not designs: addition without composition needs stable mixed notation and operation meaning; composition needs learner-owned grouping across a whole; subtraction without decomposition needs subtraction meaning and compatible units; decomposition needs reversing a whole into units and preserving value. Representation switching, forward/backward operation movement, larger capacity, random/adaptive practice, learner tracking and multiplication/division remain separate follow-ups. No assumed instructional sequence or proficiency claim follows from exact APIs.

Owner choices, with recommendations: (1) adopt count entry with learner-owned construction as a separate short task; (2) supply improper form and app-supply mixed notation only after acceptance; (3) use [0,2] with halves and fourths initially, subject to capability checks; (4) preserve unreduced source-unit remainder and request organization for equal improper remainders; (5) adopt or revise the six-record batch above, with eighths deferred pending a separate choice and capability checks; (6) leave OQ-24 and Phase 4 disposition open pending their broader evidence, with any source implementation separately authorized. No choice is presumed adopted.

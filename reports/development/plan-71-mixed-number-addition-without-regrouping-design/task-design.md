# Mixed-number addition without regrouping — design dossier

2026-11-06. **Recommendation for technical and owner review, not adoption or implementation.** Scope approved by FF Orch 12+ after the [Requirement 0 proposal](scope-proposal.md). Source baseline: `6ce31af06f602e745ff7547fce772d88ccaf71de`; `git diff --name-only 6ce31af HEAD -- src docs/founding` was empty at investigation. This is a design judgment, not learner evidence.

## Recommendation and learner responsibility

Offer a separate short practice task: show two mixed-number operands, each as the same-size whole units plus source-denominator pieces, and ask the learner for **one sum** using two initially blank fields: whole units and remaining source pieces. One Check evaluates the combined response. After acceptance show the app-supplied grouped mixed numeral beside the retained operands and accepted response; offer shared Next or final Back. The learner determines both the whole-unit total and the fractional-unit total, and must recognize that the denominator names the size of a piece rather than a count to add. The inputs supply a fixed denominator as context; the learner does not type or independently produce the entire conventional numeral. The original quantities and draft are available together, not a precomputed combined model.

| Candidate | Decisions and feedback | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **Single response (recommended)** | Decide total whole units and total same-size pieces; enter both counts and Check once. Both draft regions reflect only entered counts. Incorrect or incomplete responses remain editable. | Smallest interaction that asks for both components; the static operand models make whole and piece quantities available without requiring a drag. The learner may calculate symbolically without using the model; no claim that attention to grouping was measured. |
| Staged grouping and sum | First identify/combine whole units, then fractional units; a later Check accepts the sum. A stage transition would expose an intermediate total and require edit/back semantics. | Makes intermediate decisions more explicit, but repeats interaction and risks supplying one component of the answer. Existing evidence does not establish a benefit that warrants the extra stage. Reconsider if observed learners need it; do not infer that from the API. |

Plan 58 instead asks for grouping of **one supplied improper fraction**, with the app presenting notation after acceptance. Its accepted interaction and equal-scale model are useful precedents, not an arithmetic task already implemented ([accepted design](../plan-54-whole-plus-fraction-construction-design/task-design.md), [owner acceptance](../plan-58-whole-plus-fraction-construction/owner-acceptance.md)). Roadmap [§§39–41](../../../docs/founding/06-roadmap.md) orders this task before composition. No comparative teaching effectiveness or prerequisite mastery has been established.

## Proposed first batch and exact checks

Each operand is a positive mixed number with a proper positive fractional part; denominators match within each item and their fractional sum is strictly below one. The displayed forms in this table use slash notation solely for compact reporting; the proposed product notation stacks each fraction and keeps each whole and fraction together.

| ID | Authored operands | Source-unit sum | Proposed result | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1 1/4 + 1 1/4 | 2/4 < 1 | 2 2/4 | Same whole and fraction parts; preserve an unreduced result. |
| B | 1 1/3 + 2 1/3 | 2/3 < 1 | 3 2/3 | Different whole counts and a different source unit. |
| C | 2 1/4 + 1 2/4 | 3/4 < 1 | 3 3/4 | Different whole and piece counts; order does not imply matching components. |
| D | 1 1/8 + 2 3/8 | 4/8 < 1 | 3 4/8 | Eighths and an unreduced half-equivalent result; check visual density before admitting it to a later implementation. |

The executable [exact evidence](evidence/exact-examples.mjs) and its [output](evidence/exact-examples.json) use `createFraction`, `addFractions`, comparison, mixed construction, conversion and `addMixedNumbers`. They assert equal denominators, proper positive fractional parts, a strict fractional sum below one, expected raw result forms, and exact equality via improper-fraction addition. At the pinned source, `addMixedNumbers` converts via exact fractions and returns a proper mixed form; for these forms its output preserves the source denominator. It does not itself define a content admission rule, an instructional model or a general promise to preserve every authored form ([mixed-number source](../../../src/math/mixed-number.js), [fraction source](../../../src/math/fraction.js)). All four checked candidates are proposed, not registered content. A later implementation must explicitly admit these identities and bounds. Halves with both positive fractional parts cannot meet this same-denominator, less-than-one condition; do not invent a halves item by admitting zero or reaching one.

Require answers in the supplied source unit for this pilot: A's 2 wholes and 2 fourths is accepted without forcing 1/2; a free-denominator equivalent response is not offered. This is a proposed task constraint, not a mathematical claim that equivalent fractions are wrong. Preserve operand forms, raw draft strings, source unit and accepted exact value separately. Count range for a bounded pilot can be 0–4 whole units and 0–d pieces, subject to checking maximum draft rendering; d pieces is representable so a wrong answer is not silently normalized. All four correct remainders are strictly below d. Incomplete, malformed and out-of-range entries get neutral actionable input guidance; valid unequal amounts get a short retry, with no automatic correction. A numerically equal but improperly organized draft, if admitted by these bounds, gets neutral organization guidance, not an incorrect-arithmetic claim; classification and exact-equivalence policy need specification in a later mechanism gate. Do not simplify or convert the learner's draft before evaluation.

## Presentation, journeys, and help

Prompt: **“Add these amounts.”** Render each operand as one unbroken whole-plus-stacked-fraction group, joined by plus. Below each, show whole rows and a separate partially filled row partitioned into its denominator; all rows in both operands use the same physical whole width and equal partition scale, labeled First amount and Second amount. These are representations of the *given operands*, not a combined answer. Two editable controls follow: **“Whole units in the sum”** and **“[Fourth/third/eighth] pieces in the sum.”** Fixed unit labeling belongs to the field and its accessible name. The draft region summarizes only entered values, with blank as “not entered,” not zero; its count model may show a full row for each entered whole and a separate source-piece row for entered pieces. Input updates must preserve focus/caret, and Check must evaluate the current revision. Keep the operands visible so the learner can inspect what is being added.

**A, correct:** read 1 and one fourth plus 1 and one fourth; enter 2 whole units and 2 fourth pieces; inspect the tentative draft and Check. Show a brief success message, accepted count model and app-supplied grouped 2 with stacked 2/4; Next is directly available. No automatic simplification to 2 1/2 and no claim the learner typed mixed notation.

**C, revise with help:** for 2 and one fourth plus 1 and two fourths, enter 3 wholes and 2 fourth pieces and Check. Say “Check both amounts and try again”; retain the draft. On request, Help says “Look at the whole units in each amount. Then count the fourth pieces in both amounts.” It may highlight the two original whole regions and then the two original piece regions, without displaying totals. The learner closes Help, changes 2 to 3 pieces, and checks; success reveals app-supplied 3 with stacked 3/4. Help remains part of the attempt's assistance history even after its panel closes; a fresh attempt is a separate lifecycle.

Visual highlighting must have a linear equivalent: expose the same labeled original operand quantities and the instruction to consider the whole units and source pieces; do not state computed totals. Linear participation uses the same two editable fields, Check, help, retry and success information. Its draft summary describes only entered quantities, including “not entered.” No dragging, visual counting, color or animation is required for the mathematical decisions. Visual and linear paths need not be perceptually identical; equality of information and decisions is a requirement for later verification, not a demonstrated accessibility outcome. Use coherent names such as “two and one fourth,” associated labels, visible focus, a single deliberate feedback announcement and reduced-motion equivalents. Help is optional and local, never a prefilled answer. More/Less should initially be the same for this task unless a distinct useful aid is separately designed; disclose applicability truthfully. Do not infer independence from no recorded help ([adopted support contract](../plan-56-task-specific-support-and-independence-design/support-contract.md)).

## Layout and evidence limits

Unmeasured narrow sketch: operand groups wrap onto separate lines; First amount rows stack, then Second amount rows, then vertically stacked inputs, draft, Help and Check. A Chromebook-width sketch may place operand panels side by side if every whole stays the same width; otherwise stack. Keep each mixed numeral unbroken and allow surrounding lines to reflow. Ensure one source row remains visibly a whole in both operands and the draft; do not shrink eighths independently, clip pieces or silently omit counts. A maximum proposed 4-whole-plus-d-piece draft adds five rows beyond the given operands; if unwieldy, use a complete labeled exact count summary instead of a clipped graphic, retaining input and the original operand models. This fallback describes the learner's input and is not a solved sum.

Plan 58's [progress evidence](../plan-58-whole-plus-fraction-construction/progress.md) measured four same-width draft rows in headless Chrome at 360×740 and 1093×614, plus a CSS 200% root-font case, without detected horizontal overflow in those cases. That evidence covers a different single-quantity task and at most fourths; it does not establish this denser two-operand/eighths layout. Later verify initial, partial, maximum, wrong/help and accepted views at those sizes and enlarged text; inspect full quantities, unit-scale geometry, wrapped notation, reachability and horizontal overflow. Actual browser zoom, physical Chromebook/touch/IME, real assistive-technology speech and learner comprehension require separate observations. Do not equate the existing CSS-font probe with browser zoom.

## Future source slice, compatibility and owner choices

If adopted, a separate implementation packet should first specify a task/set with these exact identities and source forms; exact admission, result classification and bounded response; one authoritative draft and help/provenance owner; pure scene withholding; and focused rendering. The existing [Plan 58 episode](../../../src/interaction/plan58-whole-plus-fraction-episode.js) and [scene](../../../src/interaction/plan58-whole-plus-fraction-scene.js) are tied to one source form, fixed bounds and accepted grouping; their state and renderer cannot be assumed to handle two operands, thirds, eighths or four-whole drafts. Reuse shared notation and representation only where verified. Keep mathematical truth in the exact core; rendering projects validated state. Specify raw entry/blank, edit revision, retry/help, reset, stale callbacks, replay compatibility and accepted notation provenance at the mechanism gate. No schema number is assigned here.

At this baseline the [session manifest and admission](../../../src/app/magnitude-session.js) enumerate comparison and distance entries and their completion rules; this task is not eligible merely by being registered. Decide separately whether any future session should include it, with task preparation, completion, lifecycle, support and exit behavior explicitly reviewed. The [local comparison record controller](../../../src/app/comparison-record-controller.js) admits a particular comparison definition and condition; this task has no record coverage by that route. A future decision must address whether it should be recorded at all, what task and assistance observations would mean, opt-in/local privacy and old-record compatibility. No observation is not evidence of unassisted performance. This dossier authorizes neither session admission nor record extension.

Future discriminating checks should reject a batch item with mismatched units or fractional sum at least one; catch denominator addition, automatic reduction or source-form loss; show no target sum in the active scene or Help; distinguish blank from zero and invalid from wrong; preserve a wrong draft and help history on revision; compare visual and linear information/decisions; catch a whole-width mismatch or clipped maximum draft; and reject stale edits after reset. Reconstruct old replays without changing their meaning. Mounted checks and actual AT/learner observations are separate evidence, not implied by arithmetic assertions.

Owner choices: (1) adopt or revise the one-response task and whether its two count decisions are sufficiently meaningful; (2) adopt the four forms, especially whether eighths merit the layout burden or should be deferred; (3) adopt fixed source-unit entry and unsimplified results; (4) approve the help and equivalent access responsibilities; (5) decide a separately gated source slice, with session and records left for separate decisions. If evidence for a pedagogical winner becomes necessary, formulate a population- and task-specific question before any targeted research. No research expansion was needed to make this limited recommendation. No source behavior, owner adoption, learner efficacy, Phase 4 closure or deployment is claimed.

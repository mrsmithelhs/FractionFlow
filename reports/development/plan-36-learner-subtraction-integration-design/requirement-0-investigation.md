# Plan 36 Requirement 0 — Investigation and Scope Recommendation

**Stage:** Requirement 0 investigation only. This dossier records evidence and a proposed scope for the next design stage; it does not adopt a pedagogy, authorize source work, or choose a representation for FractionFlow. The positive-only boundary recommendation below is the initial investigation proposal; the parent-approved design scope now requires a renewed comparison with inclusion of zero. Neither proposal is owner adoption.

## Finding

Subtraction is already mathematically exact and represented in curated content, but the active episode grammar and both learner renderer paths currently frame the operation response as adding/combining like units. There is no admitted subtraction episode, authored subtraction recipe, subtraction scene meaning, or learner subtraction route. Plan 15 provides technically accepted standalone takeaway and comparison screens, but no learner observation and no owner preference between them. Thus the integration gap is instructional and representational, not arithmetic.

## Rival-design table

| Candidate first-task fit | Learning purpose and current support | Remaining uncertainty | Meaningful observation that would count against this first-task fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Takeaway: remove from one starting amount; report what remains.** | Makes subtraction an action on a stable minuend: the second quantity tells which equal parts to mark as taken away; the learner contributes both the removal action and the remaining amount. This aligns directly with the literal “what remains?” question and Plan 15's tested segment-removal mechanism. | Repeated one-part controls may make larger subtrahends tedious. Learners may count the marks rather than connect them to the second operand, confuse crossed-out parts with lost denominator units, or find the proposed hatch inaccessible. The screen evidence does not show that learners interpret the marks as removal. | In a think-aloud or brief task observation, after the learner can operate the control, they repeatedly cannot say what the starting whole is, cannot connect the marked amount to the subtrahend, count marked parts as the answer, or treat the denominator as shrinking; the pattern persists after one concise clarification and appears across more than one child/item. A lone hesitation triggers inspection, not rejection. |
| **Comparison: align two quantities on the same whole; identify the distance/gap.** | Makes subtraction a difference between two magnitudes, retaining both quantities visibly and marking the gap. This supports the distinct question “how much more/farther?” and Plan 15's one-action gap mark. | Could obscure the removal action the initial task intends; learners may interpret the bracket as another add-on or not know which endpoint is the minuend. Aligned bars and the visual bracket may be demanding in linear/screen-reader form. No comparative learner evidence exists. | Learners repeatedly cannot identify the larger/start amount or explain the bracket as the amount between the two quantities, instead reporting one bar or treating the bracket as an added quantity; unresolved after concise clarification across more than one child/item. A single misread should prompt closer inspection. |
| **Symbolic-only: ask for the difference from a subtraction equation.** | Keeps the task narrowly on same-denominator numerator subtraction and preserves mathematical answer responsibility; it avoids choosing between visual stories. | It supplies no visual account of why the difference is the answer and is poorly aligned with FractionFlow's bridge from visual meaning to symbolic work. Existing linear path currently presents symbols but its response labels/recovery still teach addition. | If the intended first task is to make subtraction meaning visible, a learner who can enter a numerically correct answer but cannot explain what the quantities or result mean is evidence that symbol-only is insufficient. It could remain a later purposeful practice type if the owner wants symbolic fluency explicitly. |

These are rejection criteria for fit and follow-up design, not claims that Plan 15 can supply efficacy evidence. If the owner wants a representation comparison, a later separately scoped observation plan must include actual learners, consistent task/wording, and a way to record interpretation and access burden. It is not authorized in this stage.

## Narrow recommendation for scope confirmation

- **Proposed first task:** a like-denominator, positive-proper, nonnegative subtraction episode with one stable whole. Example seed for reasoning only: `5/8 − 2/8`; learner marks/removes two of the eight parts from the minuend and supplies the numerator and denominator of what remains. The single learner question can be “How much is left?” The task may show the original two operands and equation, but must not show an answer label before the learner response. The depicted removal is a required learner action, not a decorative animation; the learner then supplies the exact remaining fraction. This separates representation action from arithmetic response while keeping the learner responsible for the answer.
- **Representation recommendation:** take takeaway forward as the smallest provisional first-task fit because it naturally supports “what remains?” and uses a single stable amount. This is a design recommendation for the next investigation/design stage only. Comparison remains a separate difference task candidate; it is not a second mode to add to each episode. Existing accepted prototype screens do not validate this recommendation.
- **Result boundary:** recommend strictly positive differences for the first batch (`0 < subtrahend < minuend < 1`), with like denominators and no negatives. Defer equal operands/zero to a later explicit zero-task disposition. A zero case is mathematically valid but changes the visual endpoint to a wholly marked/empty remainder and the response `0/d` versus simplified `0/1`; the future contract must decide how zero is represented and which equivalent input forms are accepted. Deferral keeps a visible remainder in every first-task item and avoids implying that an empty bar answers the question without learner entry. The owner may choose otherwise.
- **Data diversity to consider only after scope confirmation:** vary denominator, starting numerator, removed numerator, remainder size, and whether the raw numerator difference simplifies; derive every result/form with exact APIs. Avoid commutativity, denominator changes, crossing whole, negative outcomes, mixed numbers, and handcrafted answer tables. No particular batch is proposed as adopted in this Requirement 0 report.
- **Candidate dossier scope after gate:** only `reports/development/plan-36-learner-subtraction-integration-design/`: next a concise task/response and recovery contract, exact authored varied batch proposal, state/scene/intent/provenance/replay map, visual and linear annotated flow, measured-layout plan, and staged implementation map. Do not expand into source/tests or a prototype redesign under this recommendation. Source revisions, schemas, definition/envelope versions, learner observation, activation and deployment remain separate approval decisions.

## Unresolved owner choices

1. Whether the owner adopts takeaway for the first focused task, requests a comparison observation first, or asks for another task purpose.
2. Whether the first task should ask specifically what remains (takeaway) or what the difference is (comparison), and whether the learner should perform segment removal before supplying a fraction.
3. Whether equal operands/zero belong in the initial bank or need a separate authored zero representation/response rule.
4. The appropriate later comparison evidence, if the owner wants an empirical representation choice. Prototype screen acceptance alone cannot answer this.
